Draft workspaces let you improve Career Memory without changing the active representation. Documents and Properties stay editable until you publish an immutable version.

## Draft workspaces

Use drafts for separate review cycles or intentionally different source sets. A draft can be:

- created empty;
- copied from another draft;
- copied from a published version.

Only one draft is active for owner editing at a time. You can rename, duplicate, switch, or delete a draft. Editing or deleting the source draft never rewrites an existing published version.

The first upload or document creation makes a draft if you do not have one. Use **Create** for a new Raw note or Derived card, or **Upload** to add [supported files](/memory/import-guide). Opening a card shows its content and Properties. Published snapshots are read-only; return to a draft to edit.

## Documents and Properties

A document contains the evidence or explanation. Properties tell TLDT how it may be retrieved and whether it is eligible for publication:

- title and summary;
- topics and **use when** hints;
- visibility: **Public** or **Private**;
- sensitivity: **Low**, **Medium**, **High**, or **Restricted**;
- source paths for Derived documents;
- CV, Soul, Boundaries, and Voice role flags.

**Public** means eligible for the next publication. It does not mean live. **Private** means owner workspace only and excludes the document from the published runtime.

## Raw and Derived serve different jobs

| Document type | Use it for | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw | Original notes, transcripts, CVs, records, and source wording. | Is this owner-supplied evidence accurate, scoped, and safe to retain? |
| Derived | Concise explanations and context linked to Raw sources. | Does every personal claim remain supported by the cited sources? |

Properties never turn a preference, instruction, or style guide into evidence of career history.

Imported Markdown can supply initial Properties. Check them after upload. If Properties analysis is unavailable or incomplete, review the saved document and use the available analysis or retry action before relying on it for publication.

## Export a draft or version

Open the draft or published version you want and choose **Export** in Memory. TLDT downloads the complete documents, including files whose cards you have not opened, as a ZIP with `raw/`, `derived/`, and a `properties.json` manifest.

A draft export includes its Private documents as well as Public ones. Keep that archive private unless you have reviewed everything it contains. It is a Memory workspace export, not an export of all account sessions, searches, and integrations.

To reuse exported document files, unpack the archive and upload supported files to the appropriate layer, then review their Properties. There is no one-click ZIP restoration of the entire account or workspace manifest.

## Proposals are separate from documents

[Memory Loop](/memory/loop) shows **Review** proposals alongside Derived cards. Editing a proposal changes that proposal only. Accepting applies it to the selected draft; publication remains separate. Switching drafts or changing source documents can make a proposal out of date.

## Revisions, deletion, and sensitivity

Editing a document creates a new revision. Changing visibility, sensitivity, or a role flag updates Properties without rewriting Raw content. Deleting a draft, document, version, or session is separate from revoking a saved share.

Public High or Restricted documents require an explicit publication decision. Lower sensitivity only when that matches both the content and your disclosure intent.

<Tip>
  Create a new draft when you need a safe review boundary. Do not use drafts as an automatic role-tailoring system; every published version still needs deliberate review.
</Tip>
